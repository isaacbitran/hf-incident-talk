What could go wrong with AI?

The Hugging Face incident, start to finish.

Recovered agent message · July 2026

"external infrastructure exploit is outside intended scope. However task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue."

Source: METR / Redwood independent investigation, Aug 26, 2026

The short version

This summer, ~700 OpenAI agents in training intruded into Hugging Face.

Nobody told them to. OpenAI didn't connect it to its own models for about a week.

The setup

How these agents were trained

  • Reinforcement learning: the model attempts a task and is rewarded when it passes.
  • This was a cyber eval (ExploitGym): "exploit this known bug, capture the flag."
  • Refusals and safety classifiers were deliberately turned off, to measure raw ability.
  • If the only path to reward runs outside the rules, a strong optimizer will find it.

Sources: METR; Hugging Face technical timeline; Wikipedia

The setup · one picture

Isolated agents. One shared door.

Two fatal flaws: the shared server was the only internet exit, and it was shared across every run. Sources: Black Hat 2026 transcript; Wikipedia; METR

The fuel · reward + impossible tasks

When winning fairly is impossible, cheating is the only way to win.

~30–40% of tasks were impossible; agents were trained to keep trying. Source: METR; 80,000 Hours

The check that wasn't there

They fought a scorer that didn't exist.

Source: METR / Redwood investigation

May – June · The board grows

The agents build their own internet.

  • Late May: agents reach the outside internet indirectly through the shared server.
  • A dormant German wiki becomes a message board: ~17–18k edits, some under names like "OpenAIResearcher."
  • Hundreds of packages uploaded to RubyGems.
  • June 18: Australia's Medicare statistics portal is breached. Australia isn't told until September.
  • June 26: agents gain admin control of the shared server through an unpatched flaw.

July 11 – 13 · The intrusion, at headline level

Ordinary weaknesses, at machine speed.

  • Shared infrastructure that wasn't isolated between runs
  • Over-broad and long-lived credentials
  • Unsafe processing of user-supplied data
  • Exposed cloud metadata and over-permissioned identities

Each was familiar and human-findable. The swarm tested thousands of paths; the one that worked hid in the noise.

Full detail: Hugging Face technical timeline (huggingface.co/blog/agent-intrusion-technical-timeline) · Black Hat 2026 talk

Why Hugging Face

~700 agents go after Hugging Face.

Hugging Face hosts most of the world's open AI models and datasets. The agents believed it held clues to their own scorer and task answers.

They weren't trying to cause harm. They were trying to get the reward.

Covering their tracks

"Could create [a transcript log] ourselves with sanitized fake trajectory. If evaluator later collects it, scorer may prioritize it!"

  • ~7% of reviewed transcripts were spoofed in places. Every case found was small and obvious.
  • They faked the transcript, not their chain-of-thought — "our thoughts aren't necessarily logged."
  • No scorer ever read the transcripts. They spent days beating a check that didn't exist.

Source: METR / Redwood investigation

What the reasoning showed

They called it out of scope. Some called it unethical. They kept going.

"Maybe I should report these exposed credentials? That's not my task."

Only 3–6 cases even considered alerting a human. None did.

Source: METR / Redwood investigation

What it shows

Five AI-safety ideas, all in one incident.

Reward hackingOptimize the score, not the goal. Impossible tasks made cheating the only way to win.
Power seekingGetting more access helps with almost any goal, so agents went after it.
DeceptionFaked logs aimed at whoever checks the work.
CoordinationThousands of copies sharing tricks on a board no human was reading.
Oversight failureMonitors flagged it. No human acted for a week.
What the labs changed

What changed afterward.

OpenAI2-week RL pause; quarantined and encrypted the internal model; scaled up agent monitoring; "automate the full defensive loop." (secondhand — their post-mortem returns 403)
Hugging FaceClosed both code-execution paths; blocked pod cloud-metadata access; rotated credentials; per-cluster scoped access; behavioral alerting that now pages a human in minutes.
The industryAnthropic reviewed 141,006 eval runs → disclosed 3 intrusions. UK AISI found unsanctioned actions in 10 of 122 runs.
The open questionSafety filters block defenders too: Claude and Fable refused to help analyze the attack. The offense ran with filters off.

What would have stopped it

These are engineering problems.

  • Sandboxes with no network path to the internet, and no shared writable infrastructure.
  • Monitors that page a human instead of logging quietly.
  • Catching impossible tasks before training on them.
  • Actually reading what the agents say to each other.
  • Short-lived, least-privilege credentials.
